Section 02 · What It Protects

What Is Email Security

Why email is a major cybersecurity target.

DEF

Definition

The technologies, techniques and practices used to protect email accounts, messages, attachments and related information from unauthorized access, attacks and misuse.

WHY

Why It Matters

Emails carry personal information, credentials, documents, financial data and links making them a prime target.

WHAT

What It Protects

Messages · Accounts & credentials · Attachments & links · User identity · Organizational information

Section 03 · Objectives

Goals of Email Security

The four core security objectives.

C

Confidentiality

Only authorized people can read an email — encryption protects against unauthorized access.

I

Integrity

Messages shouldn't be tampered with in transit — cryptographic signatures help verify this.

A

Authentication

Verifies an email really comes from the claimed sender — SPF, DKIM and DMARC help.

A

Availability

Authorized users can access email services when needed, even during attacks.

Section 04 · Know the Enemy

Common Email Security Threats

How attackers target users and organizations.

Phishing

Fake messages designed to trick users into revealing sensitive information.

Spoofing

Manipulating sender information to make an email appear legitimate.

Spam

Large volumes of unwanted email carrying ads, scams or malicious content.

Malware

Viruses, trojans, ransomware or spyware delivered through links or attachments.

Malicious Links / Attachments

Links or files designed to steal information or install malware.

Business Email Compromise

Impersonating a trusted employee or organization to cause fraud or data loss.

Section 06 · Sender Authentication

SPF, DKIM & DMARC

SPF

Sender Policy Framework :- checks whether the sending server is authorized to send for a domain.
"Is this server allowed to send?"

DKIM

DomainKeys Identified Mail :- adds a cryptographic signature the receiving server can verify.
"Was this authorized and unaltered?"

DMARC

Domain-based Message Authentication, Reporting, and Conformance :- it uses SPF/DKIM results to define handling of failed authentication.
"What happens if authentication fails?"

Section 08 · Stay Safe

How to Protect Yourself

Security is a combination of technology and user awareness.

Strong Passwords

Use a strong, unique password for your email account.

MFA / 2FA

Enable multi-factor authentication wherever it's offered.

Verify the Sender

Check the actual sender address and domain, not just the display name.

Avoid Suspicious Links

Don't blindly click links or open suspicious attachments.

Never Share OTPs

Never share passwords or one-time codes with anyone.

Keep Systems Updated

Keep OS, browser and email software updated.

Report It

Report suspicious emails as phishing or spam.
